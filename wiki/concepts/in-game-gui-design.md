# In-game GUI design

Emulating the look of a 2011-era Java game inside a browser. The lesson from implementing it
for the [[Minecraft Build Tracker]] is that authenticity comes almost entirely from **chrome
and geometry**, not from copying artwork: once the panel grey, the 2px bevel, the square corner
and the bitmap font are right, a flat CSS prototype already reads as Minecraft. Raw decisions:
[`raw/sources/2026-10-25-minecraft-gui-design-system.md`](../../raw/sources/2026-10-25-minecraft-gui-design-system.md).

## The load-bearing details

- **Panel grey `#c6c6c6`** with a **2px black outline** and an **exact 2px bevel**: white on
  top-left, `#555` on bottom-right. This single component does most of the work; a 1px or 3px
  bevel immediately looks wrong.
- **Square everything.** One `border-radius` anywhere breaks the illusion.
- **Inverted bevel for insets** (slots, pressed buttons): dark top-left, light bottom-right.
- **Bitmap type** at integer sizes, bundled locally. Since EDICT-0013 the primary face is the
  game's own **Minecraft.ttf** (Craftron Gaming, DaFont "100% Free"; 110 glyphs by the page,
  107 mapped by `cmap`, euro U+20AC present, no accented letters, arrows/dashes/ellipsis/`×`/
  `·`/`≥`/`≤` absent); **Pixelify Sans** stays the bundled fallback for those glyphs. Lowercase
  draws as small capitals — the vanilla look. The shadow is a hard 2px offset, never a blur.
- **Slots and XP bars** are semantic objects in the game's visual vocabulary: a 30×30 inset
  square holding a 22 px item render (downscaled from the game's 256 px inventory render), and
  a green bar with a lighter top band.
- **Vanilla tooltip in the top layer.** The plate is `#100010` with a `#5000ff` border, but since
  EDICT-0014 one shared `#mc-tooltip` carries every `data-tip` instead of per-slot
  `::after`/`::before` rules: it is appended to the open `<dialog>` (so `overflow` cannot clip
  it), positioned fixed, clamped to the viewport and flips below when the slot lacks room above.
- **A textured world behind the panel** (tiled dirt, darkened with a vignette) supplies the
  "in-game screen" framing that a flat colour cannot.

## Native controls versus custom emulation

A browser `<select>` popup is an OS-level window: page CSS cannot style it before Chrome's
experimental `appearance: base-select`. Rather than replace every dropdown with a custom listbox
(focus management, typeahead, keyboard traversal and platform conventions come free with the
native control), EDICT-0013 keeps `<select>` and themes it where the engine allows:
`@supports (appearance: base-select)` turns on `appearance: base-select`, and `::picker(select)`
is drawn as the panel face (`rgb(111, 111, 111)`, 2 px black border, Minecraft face). Everything
else keeps the native popup. The cost is a Chromium-155-only theme; the gain is that the control
stays a real select and no JavaScript listbox has to reimplement one ([[Minecraft Build
Tracker]]). The supporting detail is that the open popup cannot be screenshotted — the OS paints
it outside the page — so the acceptance claim rests on a computed-style probe and the `:open`
observation, not on a picture.

## Why CSS instead of a texture pack

Real GUI textures would need nine-slice scaling and hundreds of image files. Because the shapes
are simple rectangles with a fixed bevel, CSS reproduces them more faithfully at any size and
keeps the repository free of third-party art. Two exceptions need real pixels: the tiling
block textures are generated by us, and the **item icons are the game's own 256×256 inventory
renders**, exported locally from the owner's client and kept out of version control
([[Vanilla assets without redistribution]]). The tiled textures are 16×16 and scaled with
`image-rendering: pixelated`; the item renders are displayed with `image-rendering: auto`
because nearest-scaling a 256 px render into a 22 px slot aliases (EDICT-0011).

## The browser defaults are a design surface

A 2011 game had no OS scrollbar, no native tooltip clipped by `overflow` and no dead-end toast.
The EDICT-0014 lesson is that wherever the browser's default is visible, it is part of the design
whether or not it was designed:

- **One tooltip, in the top layer.** Per-slot CSS pseudo-elements looked correct until a dialog's
  `overflow` or a later sibling painted over them; the shared fixed surface fixes the stacking
  problem once, at the cost of a small JavaScript positioner and a 44 px flip threshold.
- **Paging, not a render cap.** A picker that renders 150 of 1,368 items is lying about its
  contents. Appending 120 tiles on a sentinel keeps the DOM cheap and the count hint honest
  (`showing N of 1368 — scroll for more`), and the last tile is a real, focusable target.
- **An empty screen is a control surface.** A view that only offers a toast ("Create a world
  first.") teaches nothing; five zero states each carry the next action in place.
- **Wrapping is geometry.** Item names ellipsed to fit a fixed slot are the browser silently
  editing the copy; wrapping 2–3 lines keeps the panel's dimensions honest at 1280 and 390 px.

## Measurement is the new screenshot pass

EDICT-0015 turned three previously visual judgements into rect arithmetic:

- **Layout alignment.** Labels pair with their own control, not one shared left edge; the rule is
  ±1 px at 1280×900 and 390×844, one control height per visual row, add-form slot/Needed/Got
  tops equal, table head and row cells aligned, and top-bar controls centred across a row. It is
  asserted with `getBoundingClientRect` against an injected fixture, not judged from a screenshot
  ([[Testing a UI like a user]]).
- **Height as a budget.** The desktop page must fit 900 px exactly and the 390×844 page ≤1,500
  (measured 1,423); only material lists may scroll. The budget fixture (4 builds, 6 materials) is
  test data, not `?demo=1`, so the number means the same thing on every run.
- **State that can be derived.** The world header's `"X% gathered"` is computed from
  `M.worldSummary` on each render; a literal would be a second source of truth
  ([[Status-ranked build ordering]]).
- **Dialogs for file paths.** Import got a real dialog (`accept=".json"`, drop, paste, restore)
  because Chrome's chooser used the first `accept` token as its extension filter and this machine
  has no `.json` MIME type — a browser fact, not a design preference.
- **A control for motion.** One `.backdrop__layer` at 4:1 (`width: 800vh; background-size: 400vh
  auto; repeat-x`) drifts −50 % over 120 s; `#backdrop-pause` is the WCAG 2.2.2 stop, and
  `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` defaults the layer to paused rather than removing it — the OS
  preference sets the initial state, the control changes it.

## Tensions

- **Contrast vs. fidelity.** Vanilla draws white text on light grey, which fails WCAG AA. The
  shipped system uses dark text on light panels and shadowed white text on dark rows. It looks
  right and it is readable; it is not pixel-identical ([[Minecraft Build Tracker]]).
- **Legibility at small sizes.** True Minecraft text at 8–10 px is unreadable on a modern
  display; the app uses 13–16 px pixel type instead.
- **Nostalgia is a moving target.** "Looks like Minecraft" spans 1.7 to 1.21, each with a
  different GUI palette. This system targets the grey-panel era deliberately.
- **A bitmap face constrains copy.** The game's font maps 107 codepoints — ASCII, euro and a
  little Latin-1 — and lacks arrows, dashes, ellipsis, `×`, `·`, `≥`, `≤`; those characters fall
  back to Pixelify Sans, so any copy using them mixes two faces. The alternative is refusing
  valid UI text to protect a purer look; the shipped choice favours the text.
- **Native theming is a browser fact, not a design decision.** The themed dropdown path needs
  `appearance: base-select` (Chrome 155 in this build). If the product must look identical in
  Firefox and Safari, the only options are a JavaScript listbox or the unthemed OS popup — the
  design system currently ships the split and documents it.
- **Taste is measurable only up to a point.** The backdrop is now defined by numbers (six inputs,
  four stitched sides, 2,560×640, bake 2.0, CSS 1 px, 0.2 scrim, one 800vh layer over a 120 s
  drift, a pause control), but the comparison that would settle it — side by side with the real
  title screen on a physical display — has not been made ([[Open questions]]).
- **Seamless is a loop, not a video.** One `.backdrop__layer` 800vh wide, repeating a 400vh-at-auto
  strip and translated −50 % over 120 s, gives a clean wrap with one style rule; the cost is that
  the strip must tile perfectly and the eye sees the same four side faces every cycle. A true
  six-face orbit would need more usable source views than the release ships (the top/bottom caps
  are recorded, not stitched).
- **Motion is a preference with a control.** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` now defaults the
  layer to paused with the frame still painted, and `#backdrop-pause` (or a stored `motion=play`)
  is the opt-in — the media query sets the default, not a veto. The paused phase is not persisted:
  a reload restarts at phase 0, so the frozen frame re-samples the strip.
- **Alignment is a two-viewport claim.** 1280×900 and 390×844 are asserted pairwise; other widths
  and HiDPI zoom levels are outside the measured contract, and a long builds list is allowed to
  scroll ([[Testing a UI like a user]]).
- **Paging trades completeness for discovery.** All 1,368 items are reachable but only by
  scrolling; there is no letter jump or virtual list, so finding an obscure item depends on
  search, recents or the suggestion ([[Open questions]]).
- **The suggestion is editorial, not intelligent.** A frozen keyword table can recommend a
  plausible-looking wrong icon; the reason string makes the guess visible, and the fixture table
  is the contract — deterministic, not a model ([[Open questions]]).

## Real assets versus drawn ones

Hand-drawn approximations got the layout right but not the recognition: a familiar ingot or
cobblestone cube is what makes a slot read instantly. The shipped set therefore uses the
game's own **inventory renders** — 256×256 images from the game's item renderer, exported with
IconExporter — with the legal and technical machinery documented separately
([[Vanilla assets without redistribution]]). The renderer matters: an early extractor
composited cube icons from 16×16 textures by hand and the geometry was visibly off (skewed
logs, flat stand-ins for 3D items); the game's own projection fixed that and raised the
resolution in the same move. That 256 px source is then shown at UI sizes with smooth
downscaling — the one place the pixel-art "integer scale" rule is deliberately retired.

One more trap: the exporter's own output was **darker than the game it came from**.
IconExporter 1.3.1 scales the icon pose non-uniformly, which flattens Minecraft's GUI diffuse
lighting, so 3D block icons exported roughly a third too dark while flat sprites stayed
correct. The tracker now restores the vanilla inventory face brightness (0.99 / 0.65 / 0.40 of
the block texture for top / left / right, calibrated against the jar and recorded in the
manifest) — a documented compensation for an upstream bug, not a re-render
([[Vanilla assets without redistribution]]). The lesson for design systems: "use the game's
own asset" only guarantees authenticity if the extraction path is verified against the game's
own appearance; a reference measurement belongs in the acceptance criteria.

## Panorama backdrop

The menu's atmosphere is not the GUI chrome — it is the panorama behind it: six cube-face
screenshots of a world, blurred and slowly rotating. The app resolves six inputs, stitches the
four side faces with a wrap crossfade (the caps are recorded but not stitched), bakes at a
Gaussian radius of **2.0** and composes a 2,560×640 strip (55,096 B, sha256 `331253bd…bf1a5`).
Display is a single `.backdrop__layer` 800vh wide (`background-size: 400vh auto`, repeat-x)
translated −50 % over **120 s** linear infinite, with CSS `blur(1px)` (1.5 px at ≤640 px) and a
**0.2** scrim; `#backdrop-pause` stops it, and `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` defaults the layer
to paused with the frame painted (the OS preference is respected; the layer never gets
`animation: none`). EDICT-0013's sharper
bake answered the emperor's "less blurred so you can tell what it is": edge-variance on the same
1,280×900 strip rose from 1,132.6 to 1,379.9 and the strip grew from 15,280 to 45,664 B; EDICT-0014
made the rotation seamless and settled the geometry. Crucially, the source is odd: the version
jar's `panorama_*.png` files are 1×1 placeholders; the real 1024×1024 faces live in the asset
store and are resolved through the asset index. Identity avatars sit at the other end of the
same idea: small, square, bevelled slots that make a world or build recognisable at a glance
([[Atmosphere and identity without shipping assets]]).
